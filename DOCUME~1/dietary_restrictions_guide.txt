VEGETARIAN AND VEGAN OPTIONS

Roadrunner Café has a dedicated vegan station near the back of the dining hall. This station uses separate utensils and is generally considered reliable for avoiding cross-contamination. The menu at the vegan station rotates but typically includes a grain bowl, roasted vegetables, and a plant-based protein option. Students report this station is the safest bet for vegans.

The salad bar at Roadrunner Café is vegetarian-friendly but not strictly vegan due to cheese and dairy being accessible in the same area. Confirmed vegan items at the salad bar: fresh vegetables, chickpeas, kidney beans, sunflower seeds, and oil-and-vinegar dressings. Ranch and most creamy dressings contain dairy.

"I'm vegan and I eat at Roadrunner Café about 4 times a week. The dedicated vegan station is solid. I've had one incident where something was mislabeled but when I flagged it the staff were responsive and fixed the signage. The bigger issue is variety — it's the same 2-3 items on rotation. I supplement with the salad bar." — vegan_utsa_junior

"The stir-fry station can accommodate vegetarians and vegans if you specify — they'll use a clean wok and skip the meat. Ask for tofu if it's available that day. Not every day has tofu." — vegetarian_utsa

GLUTEN-FREE OPTIONS

Gluten-free eating at UTSA dining is challenging. Roadrunner Café does not have a dedicated gluten-free station, and cross-contamination is a real risk because the same serving utensils are often used across stations. Students with celiac disease are strongly advised to contact Dining Services directly before relying on the dining hall.

Confirmed safer options for gluten-free students at Roadrunner Café: the salad bar (avoid croutons), plain grilled proteins from the grill station (ask for them to be cooked on a clean surface), and fresh fruit. Most sauces, dressings, and gravies should be assumed to contain gluten unless labeled otherwise.

"I have celiac and I met with Dining Services at the start of each semester. They're genuinely helpful — they gave me a guide to what was safe and flagged a staff member I could check with when I had questions. You have to advocate for yourself though; the general setup is not celiac-safe without those accommodations." — celiac_utsa_student

At the Sombrilla, most chain restaurants (Chick-fil-A, Panda Express) have online allergen menus available through their national websites. These apply to campus locations but cross-contamination procedures may differ.

HALAL AND KOSHER OPTIONS

UTSA dining does not offer a dedicated halal or kosher dining facility. Some students report finding halal-suitable options at Roadrunner Café (fish dishes, vegetarian options) but there is no halal certification for the dining hall as a whole.

For halal food on or near campus: the Rowdy Cart occasionally has halal-certified meat options (check their daily posts on Instagram @UTSADining). Off campus, the closest reliable halal restaurants are approximately 10-15 minutes by car — there are several options on Loop 1604 and near the North Star Mall area.

"As a Muslim student I mostly eat vegetarian at Roadrunner Café and supplement with off-campus halal spots. There's a halal food truck that parks near the engineering building on Thursdays that several Muslim students on campus rely on." — muslim_student_utsa

NUT ALLERGIES

Tree nut and peanut allergens are present throughout the dining hall. No nut-free stations exist. If you have a severe nut allergy, contact Dining Services — they have protocols including separate preparation areas and can work with you on a case-by-case basis. The Jamba Juice in the Sombrilla contains tree nuts in many smoothies and cross-contamination is likely.

---

HOW TO GET ACCOMMODATIONS

Email dining@utsa.edu with your dietary restriction and student ID number before the semester starts. They typically respond within 2-3 business days. Accommodations can include: a guided tour of the dining hall with a manager, a list of safe daily options, and a point of contact for questions during the semester.

"I emailed them before my freshman year about a shellfish allergy and they were really thorough. They walked me through every station and showed me where shellfish was likely to appear. It took 30 minutes and I felt a lot more confident after." — allergy_utsa_freshman
