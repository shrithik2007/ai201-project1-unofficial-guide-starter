THE PLANS (as of Fall 2023):

Unlimited Plan: $2,450/semester — Unlimited swipes at Roadrunner Café + $100 Dining Dollars
Block 160 Plan: $2,050/semester — 160 meal swipes + $200 Dining Dollars
Block 120 Plan: $1,750/semester — 120 meal swipes + $150 Dining Dollars
Commuter Plan: $500/semester — 0 swipes + $500 Dining Dollars (usable anywhere)

Dining Dollars vs. Rowdy Bucks: Dining Dollars are loaded onto your 1501 card as part of a meal plan, expire at end of semester, and can only be used at campus dining locations. Rowdy Bucks are a general spending account loaded onto the same card, don't expire, and can be used anywhere that accepts 1501 card. When you "add money to your 1501 card" without specifying, it usually goes to Rowdy Bucks.

---

Student takes on which plan to choose:

"If you live on campus and are required to have a meal plan, get the Unlimited. Yes it costs more but you will not regret having the flexibility. Running out of meal swipes mid-semester is a real thing that happens to people on block plans and then you're paying $14.50 cash per meal." — fourth_year_utsa

"I'm on Block 160 and it's fine if you're disciplined. That's roughly 10 swipes per week for 16 weeks. The issue is you get sick of Roadrunner Café fast. I only use about 7-8 swipes per week and supplement with food from home. The Dining Dollars on this plan are enough to cover a couple Chick-fil-A meals per week." — junior_engineering

"Do NOT get the Block 120 unless you commute. 120 swipes is 7.5 per week which barely covers one meal a day. You'll burn through them faster than you think during first few weeks when everything is novel." — sophomore_advice_2023

"The Commuter Plan is actually really smart for commuters who eat on campus 2-3 days a week. $500 in Dining Dollars goes further than it sounds because you're not locked into the $14.50 swipe value — you only pay for what you actually eat at retail locations." — commuter_utsa_23

MEAL SWIPE TIPS:

Swipes reset every week for block plans — they do NOT roll over. If you have unused swipes at the end of the week, you lose them. Use them or lose them.

Rowdy Bucks and Dining Dollars DO roll over within the semester but Dining Dollars expire at semester end. Rowdy Bucks never expire.

You can use one meal swipe per visit at Roadrunner Café. There's a 30-minute lockout — you can't swipe in again within 30 minutes of your last swipe. This matters if you finish fast and want to go back.

Guest swipes: You can use a meal swipe to bring one guest into Roadrunner Café. You swipe for them at the entrance. Most plans include 5 guest swipes per semester.

COMMON MISTAKES:

"I loaded my money onto Dining Dollars instead of Rowdy Bucks and then they expired at the end of May. Lost about $80. Make sure you know the difference." — warning_from_a_sophomore

"I thought the Chick-fil-A and Panda Express in the Sombrilla accepted meal swipes. They don't. Only Roadrunner Café takes swipes. Everything else in the Sombrilla is Dining Dollars or Rowdy Bucks." — new_student_2023

"I tried to use my meal plan at the food trucks near Engineering. Those are independent trucks — they take cash or card only, not your 1501 card at all." — freshman_mistake

"The Dining Dollars I had left at the end of fall semester didn't transfer to spring. UTSA doesn't carry them over between semesters. Spent $40 at Jamba Juice in the last week of finals because I had to burn them." — advice_for_seniors

IS THE MEAL PLAN WORTH IT?

"For on-campus residents it's basically required and the Unlimited plan is the best value per meal if you actually use it. If you're eating there twice a day you're paying about $7-8 per meal equivalent which is pretty good for San Antonio." — ra_perspective

"For commuters: no, the mandatory meal plan doesn't apply to you and you should only get one if you genuinely think you'll eat on campus 3+ times per week. The food trucks and off-campus options are better value." — commuter_senior

"The plan is worth it for the social aspect freshman year as much as the food. Roadrunner Café is where you meet people. After sophomore year most people move off campus and cook for themselves." — honest_upperclassman
